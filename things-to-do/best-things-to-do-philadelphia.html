The Best Things to Do in Philadelphia

Founding-era history, world-class museums, great markets and one-of-a-kind experiences — the essential Philadelphia checklist.

GPBy the GuidePhilly team · Updated July 2026
Advertisement728 × 90 · Monetag zone

Philadelphia rewards curiosity. It's the birthplace of American democracy, a serious arts and food town, and a city of distinct, walkable neighborhoods all packed into a compact core.

Whether you have a day or a long weekend, these are the attractions and experiences worth your time.

1

Liberty Bell

The world's most famous symbol of American freedom

Historic SiteOld CityFree
Liberty Bell

Housed in its own glass-walled center, the cracked bronze bell draws visitors from around the globe to trace its journey from colonial statehouse fixture to an emblem of abolition and independence. Free timed entry and a walk-through exhibit make it an easy, moving first stop in Philadelphia's historic core.

Address: 526 Market St, Philadelphia, PA 19106

2

Independence Hall

The birthplace of American independence

Historic SiteOld CityFree
Independence Hall

Both the Declaration of Independence and the U.S. Constitution were debated and signed inside this UNESCO World Heritage brick building, and ranger-led tours bring the founding-era rooms vividly to life. Admission is free but requires a timed ticket, so it pays to reserve ahead during busy months.

Address: 520 Chestnut St, Philadelphia, PA 19106
Visit website ↗Get directions ↗
3

Philadelphia Museum of Art

World-class art and the famous 'Rocky' steps

MuseumBenjamin Franklin Parkway$$
Philadelphia Museum of Art

One of the largest art museums in the country holds more than 240,000 works spanning Van Gogh, medieval armor and a reconstructed Japanese teahouse. Even non-visitors climb its grand east steps to strike a triumphant pose beside the Rocky statue and take in the skyline view.

Address: 2600 Benjamin Franklin Pkwy, Philadelphia, PA 19130
Visit website ↗Get directions ↗
4

Reading Terminal Market

Philly's historic food hall since 1893

MarketCenter CityFree
Reading Terminal Market

More than a hundred vendors pack this bustling indoor market, serving everything from Amish soft pretzels and roast pork sandwiches to fresh produce and international street food. It is the best single place in the city to graze your way through Philadelphia's culinary identity in one visit.

Address: 1136 Arch St, Philadelphia, PA 19107
Visit website ↗Get directions ↗
5

Eastern State Penitentiary

The world's first true penitentiary, now a haunting ruin

Historic SiteFairmount$$
Eastern State Penitentiary

Once the most famous and expensive prison in the world, this crumbling castle-like fortress held Al Capone and pioneered the controversial solitary-confinement model. Self-guided audio tours through its decaying cellblocks are gripping by day, and the site transforms into a legendary haunted attraction each fall.

Address: 2027 Fairmount Ave, Philadelphia, PA 19130

6

Italian Market

America's oldest continuously operating outdoor market

MarketSouth PhiladelphiaFree
Italian Market

Stretching along 9th Street, this open-air market has fed Philadelphia for over a century with fresh pasta, cheeses, produce, spices and old-school Italian delis. Wandering its curb-side stalls and family-run shops delivers a taste of the city's deep immigrant roots and enduring food culture.

Address: 919 S 9th St, Philadelphia, PA 19147
Visit website ↗Get directions ↗
7

Elfreth's Alley

The nation's oldest continuously inhabited residential street

LandmarkOld CityFree
Elfreth's Alley

This cobblestone lane of 18th-century row houses, still lived in today, feels like stepping straight into colonial Philadelphia. Visitors stroll past flower boxes and gas-style lamps, while a small museum house offers a closer look at the everyday lives of early tradespeople.

Address: 126 Elfreth's Alley, Philadelphia, PA 19106
Visit website ↗Get directions ↗
8

Barnes Foundation

One of the world's greatest Impressionist collections

MuseumBenjamin Franklin Parkway$$
Barnes Foundation

Dr. Albert Barnes assembled a staggering trove of Renoir, Cezanne, Matisse and Picasso, displayed in his signature salon-style ensembles that pair fine art with ironwork and furniture. The purpose-built Parkway galleries preserve his idiosyncratic arrangements exactly as he intended.

Address: 2025 Benjamin Franklin Pkwy, Philadelphia, PA 19130
Visit website ↗Get directions ↗
9

The Franklin Institute

A hands-on temple of science and discovery

MuseumLogan Square$$
The Franklin Institute

Named for Philadelphia's favorite polymath, this beloved science museum lets visitors walk through a giant two-story model heart, explore space and electricity exhibits, and catch shows in its planetarium. Its interactive, family-friendly galleries make complex science genuinely fun for all ages.

Address: 222 N 20th St, Philadelphia, PA 19103
Visit website ↗Get directions ↗
10

Please Touch Museum

Philadelphia's premier children's museum

MuseumFairmount Park$$
Please Touch Museum

Set inside the grand 1876 Centennial Memorial Hall, this playful museum invites young kids to learn through hands-on exhibits, role-play spaces and a restored antique carousel. It is purpose-built for imaginative, unstructured play, making it a top pick for families with little ones.

Address: 4231 Avenue of the Republic, Philadelphia, PA 19131
Visit website ↗Get directions ↗

11

Philadelphia's Magic Gardens

An immersive mosaic labyrinth of found-object art

ExperienceSouth Street$
Philadelphia's Magic Gardens

Artist Isaiah Zagar covered half a city block with dazzling mosaics of tile, mirror, bottles and bicycle wheels, creating a maze-like indoor-outdoor wonderland. Every corner reveals new detail, making it one of the city's most photographed and singular creative spaces.

Address: 1020 South St, Philadelphia, PA 19147
Visit website ↗Get directions ↗
12

Spruce Street Harbor Park

A seasonal riverfront oasis of hammocks and lights

ExperiencePenn's LandingFree
Spruce Street Harbor Park

Each warm season the Delaware River waterfront fills with colorful LED-lit hammocks, floating gardens, boardwalk games and food-and-drink vendors. It is a laid-back, free-to-enter spot to relax by the water with the Ben Franklin Bridge glowing overhead.

Address: 121 N Columbus Blvd, Philadelphia, PA 19106
Visit website ↗Get directions ↗
13

Museum of the American Revolution

Home of George Washington's original war tent

MuseumOld City$$
Museum of the American Revolution

This modern museum immerses visitors in the full story of America's founding through rare artifacts, life-size dioramas and powerful multimedia experiences. Its centerpiece is the tent Washington used as his mobile headquarters, revealed in a dramatic theater presentation.

Address: 101 S 3rd St, Philadelphia, PA 19106
Visit website ↗Get directions ↗
14

LOVE Park

Home of Robert Indiana's iconic LOVE sculpture

LandmarkCenter CityFree
LOVE Park

Officially John F. Kennedy Plaza, this revamped public square is anchored by the bright red LOVE statue that has become one of Philadelphia's most recognizable photo spots. With its fountain, seating and views toward the Parkway, it is a lively gathering place in the heart of the city.

Address: 1599 John F. Kennedy Blvd, Philadelphia, PA 19102
